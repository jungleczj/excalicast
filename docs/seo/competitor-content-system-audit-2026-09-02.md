## Research evidence and limits

- The local SEMrush snapshot identifies the same product-led concentration: Loom, ScreenPal, ScreenRec, and Bandicam each have a recorder/product hub among their strongest pages; Screencastify is more brand- and education-led. See [the 2026-09-01 competitive research](semrush-competitive-research-2026-09-01.md), especially its “16 个头部工具的 SEO 做法” and Top 3 structure sections.
- Public page inspection covered the URLs linked in each row. The page-level observations are structural (navigation, headings, media, CTAs, related paths), not estimates of traffic. The round-4 Top Pages table below separately records live URL-level estimated organic traffic from the authenticated SEMrush report, dated Sep 1, 2026 and viewed Sep 2, 2026.
- Dynamic pages can change cards, dates, and navigation without notice. Recheck URLs, pricing, recorder limits, and Chrome/Google integrations before publishing a derivative Excalicast page. Mobile observations are responsive-layout spot checks, not device-lab performance measurements.
- No competitor copy is reproduced. Borrow/avoid notes are implementation guidance for Excalicast’s own examples, screenshots, voice, and product boundaries.

## Shared patterns to implement

Ranked by the overlap of the ten-brand matrix, visible public-page checks, and directional SEMrush evidence. These are Excalicast rules, not a request to reproduce competitor copy or product boundaries.

1. **Hub-and-spoke structure.** Build durable hubs for screen recording, whiteboard animation, audio/captions, and video-to-handout, with spokes for platform/device, job, format, comparison, and troubleshooting intent. A hub explains the category and routes to the next job; a spoke links back to its hub and at least two adjacent spokes. Do not create near-duplicate pages just to vary a platform noun.
2. **Device/how-to pages.** Keep one intent per page, put the direct answer before background, and show the exact UI, permissions, audio source, and current limitation for Windows, macOS, browser, and mobile cases. Merge overlapping Windows queries when the workflow is the same; split only when steps or decisions genuinely differ.
3. **Free utilities.** Use a utility only when it completes a real task without an account wall: webcam/mic test, aspect-ratio calculator, caption-file validator, or recording checklist. Give each utility a result, a next action, and a link into the relevant guide/product flow; label limits and data handling plainly.
4. **Comparison content.** Target alternatives and category comparisons with a dated method, a decision table, scenario fit, and “not a fit” boundaries. Compare production model, drawing control, audio/camera, captions, aspect ratios, collaboration, licensing, watermark, and export—not a pile of unverifiable superlatives.
5. **Template/example content.** Use templates, example recordings, annotated frames, and before/after clips as visual proof. Every indexed template or public example needs a unique explanation, permission/privacy status, and a path back to the matching how-to; a gallery without context is not a content cluster.
6. **Freshness/provenance.** Display author or reviewer, verified date, product/version or OS scope, and official sources near claims that can change. Recheck permissions, shortcuts, integrations, limits, pricing, and AI behavior at least quarterly; cite primary research for education or retention claims and disclose vendor-reported evidence.
7. **Internal links.** Link by the reader’s next job: define → choose → set up → record → edit → caption → export → publish → reuse. Use contextual anchors, related-cluster cards, breadcrumbs, and a restrained footer; avoid repeating the same exact-match anchor in every module.
8. **Conversion paths.** Match CTA to intent: utility result → try the workflow; informational guide → checklist or setup; comparison → honest fit check; template/example → duplicate or start a blank board. Keep one primary CTA above the fold and repeat it after proof, not after every paragraph. State account, permission, watermark, export, and privacy requirements before activation.

## Layout system to implement

Adopt this layout contract for new Excalicast guides, utilities, comparisons, and category pages:

- **Direct-answer hero:** H1 mirrors the job, a 40–70-word answer resolves the query, and one intent-specific CTA names the next action. Add verified date and audience/device scope when relevant.
- **Visual proof:** Place an original Excalicast frame, short replay, annotated screenshot, or before/after directly after the answer. Caption what the reader should notice; decorative stock art is not evidence.
- **Key takeaways:** Three to five bullets state the result, required inputs, important limit, and best-fit scenario before the long explanation.
- **Desktop sticky TOC / mobile disclosure TOC:** Desktop keeps a compact sticky table of contents beside the article; mobile uses a closed disclosure that opens on tap and does not consume the first screen with a giant menu.
- **Short paragraphs:** Keep most paragraphs to two or three sentences; use descriptive H2/H3s, numbered steps, and one idea per section. Pair each critical step with evidence that verifies it.
- **Decision aids:** Add a scenario matrix, “choose this if” cards, or a small comparison table whenever more than one capture source, output ratio, or workflow is plausible.
- **Reality-check callouts:** Use a distinct callout for permissions, browser/OS limits, local-versus-cloud processing, unsupported media, watermark/export constraints, and cases where Excalicast is not the right tool.
- **FAQ:** End the instructional body with four to eight intent-derived questions. Answers resolve objections or edge cases rather than repeating the hero; expose FAQ structured data only when visible answers are present.
- **Sources:** Provide title, publisher, URL, and checked date. Separate primary docs, research, and vendor claims; never turn a competitor marketing line into an Excalicast fact.
- **Related cluster:** Finish with three to six links grouped by next job (setup, device, audio, editing, publishing, comparison), each with a useful label and no duplicate near-intent slug.
- **Intent-specific CTA:** Close with the action promised by the page—open a blank board, run a test, follow a setup, or inspect a comparison. Repeat the primary CTA after visual proof and before the FAQ only when the user has enough information to act.

## Voice system to implement

Five reusable tone rules:

1. **Answer before charm.** Lead with the practical result; let personality arrive in the example or transition, never in place of the answer.
2. **Warm, concrete, and bilingual by intent.** Use plain verbs, name the actual control or output, and write Chinese as natural task language rather than a literal English shadow.
3. **Gentle workflow humor.** Acknowledge familiar friction (“the permission dialog has opinions”) without mocking beginners, teachers, accessibility needs, or a competitor’s users.
4. **Earn confidence through boundaries.** Say what was tested, what depends on browser/device, and what Excalicast cannot do; avoid “magic,” “effortless,” and unqualified “best.”
5. **Invite the next small action.** End sections with a low-pressure handoff—check one setting, draw one frame, record one take, or compare one scenario—then make the CTA match it.
